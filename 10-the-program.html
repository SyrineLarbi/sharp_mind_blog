Sharp Mind 10 · making it permanent

The program. Fifteen minutes a day, for eight weeks.

This is the shortest file in the library and the only one that asks you to do something every day. Everything else here is knowledge. This is the part that turns it into behaviour — and without it, you will have read nineteen thousand lines and changed nothing, which is the normal outcome and the one this file exists to prevent.

Part I · Before you start

Two short sections, and then the work

The first explains why this file has to exist. The second gives you a number to measure against, which matters more than it sounds.

The Program 01

Why the reading changed nothing

In one sentence

You have now read a great deal about this, and almost nothing about you has changed. That is normal, predictable, and not your fault.

Think of it like…

Reading about swimming.

You could read every book ever written about swimming — stroke mechanics, breathing, the physics of drag — and drown in three metres of water. Nobody finds this surprising about swimming.

Everybody finds it surprising about thinking, speaking and dealing with people, and it is exactly the same situation.

Why reading does not transfer, and what does
① KNOWING A MOVE AND HAVING IT AVAILABLE UNDER PRESSURE
   ARE DIFFERENT STATES OF THE SAME INFORMATION
   You have the information. It is not RETRIEVABLE at
   the moment you need it, which is the only moment
   that counts. (file 01 §01)

② EVERYTHING HERE IS INPUT UNTIL YOU HAVE SAID OR DONE
   IT
   Reading is input. Doing is stock. (file 06 §01)

③ ⭐ AND HERE IS THE SPECIFIC TRAP, BECAUSE YOU WILL
   FALL INTO IT THIS WEEK
   You will recognise the move AFTERWARDS.
   Walking out of the meeting: "I should have asked what
   would have to be true." "I hedged that whole
   sentence."
   ⇒ RECOGNISING IT AFTERWARDS IS A REAL STAGE. It is
     the stage immediately before doing it. It is also
     where most people stop, for years, and conclude
     that they know this material.
   ⇒ the gap between "recognise afterwards" and "do it
     in the moment" is closed by REPS WITH FEEDBACK.
     Nothing else closes it. (Ericsson, file 09 §—)

⭐ AND THE HONEST PROMISE, BECAUSE AN INFLATED ONE WOULD
  BE SELF-DEFEATING
  Eight weeks at fifteen minutes a day will NOT make you
  a different person. Nothing does that in eight weeks.
  What it will do is give you FOUR OR FIVE MOVES THAT
  ARRIVE WITHOUT BEING SUMMONED.
  ⇒ that is a small-sounding outcome and it is the one
    that actually changes how rooms go, because the
    moves arrive at the moment you cannot think.
The Program 02

The six skills, and scoring yourself

Do this before you read any further

Score yourself, today, on each of the six. Write the date next to it. It takes three minutes and it is the difference between knowing whether this worked and guessing.

SkillWhich fileWhat a 5 looks like
1 · Speed01 Thinking FastA hard question lands and you pause, name the category to yourself, and answer in a shape — without deciding to do any of that.
2 · Questions02 The Question BankYou routinely ask the thing nobody else asked, and you notice when a room has skipped one of the seven types.
3 · Structure03 Answer FirstYour conclusion is in the first sentence, spoken and written, without effort. Your headings are claims.
4 · Presence04 Presence and StatusYour sentences do not apologise for themselves. "I don't know, I'll have it Thursday" costs you nothing.
5 · Reading people05 Reading the RoomYou can tell what a room needs before it says so, and you adjust register per person automatically.
6 · Substance06 Having Something to SayYou are never the person standing in a group with nothing to contribute, because the shelf is stocked.
The scale · and use these words, not a feeling
1  DOESN'T HAPPEN
   I do not do this at all.

2  BY ACCIDENT
   It happens sometimes and I could not tell you why.

3  WHEN I REMEMBER
   I can do it if I think about it beforehand. I mostly
   do not think about it beforehand.

4  USUALLY, WITHOUT MUCH EFFORT
   It happens most of the time. I notice when it does
   not.

5  AUTOMATIC
   It arrives without being summoned, and I notice when
   OTHER people do not do it.

⚠ SCORE HONESTLY AND LOW. Everyone's first instinct is
  to score a 3 on everything, which measures nothing.
  If you cannot recall a specific instance from the last
  week, it is not a 4.

⭐ AND EXPECT YOUR SCORE TO GO DOWN AT WEEK FOUR ON
  AT LEAST ONE SKILL.
  This is the single most important thing in this
  section. A drop is not failure. It means you can now
  SEE something you could not see before.
  ⇒ noticing your own hedging for the first time feels
    exactly like getting worse at speaking. It is the
    opposite. You were always doing it; now you can
    hear it.
  ⇒ people who do not know this happens quit in week
    four. That is when almost everyone quits, and it is
    for this reason.
Part II · The three habits

One daily, one built once, one weekly

The daily one takes fifteen minutes. The bank takes a week to build and lasts years. The weekly one is the hardest thing in this library.

The Program 03

The daily fifteen

Three parts. Two minutes, zero minutes, five minutes.
MORNING · 2 MINUTES · THE CATEGORY CALL
  Pick the one conversation today that matters most.
  Write two lines:
    ① which of the five categories it will be —
      definition · data · trade-off · decision rights ·
      sequencing  (file 01 §05)
    ② one question you will ask  (file 02)
  ⇒ that is it. Two lines, on paper or in a note.

DURING · 0 MINUTES · THE RUNNING NOTE
  The four-symbol note from file 01 §08. You are already
  in the meeting; this costs nothing extra.
    fact        ·  the claim, three words
    number      ·  any figure said out loud
    ?  question ·  something I want to ask
    !! point    ·  something I want to say

EVENING · 5 MINUTES · THE FIVE-LINE LOG
  ⭐ THIS IS THE ONE THAT DOES THE WORK, AND IT IS THE
    ONE PEOPLE SKIP.
  ① WAS MY CATEGORY RIGHT?
    If not — what was it actually, and what was the tell
    I missed?
  ② WHAT QUESTION LANDED?
    Or: what question did I not ask that I should have?
  ③ WHAT DID I SAY THAT I'D SAY DIFFERENTLY?
    ⇒ AND WRITE THE BETTER VERSION OUT IN FULL, in the
      words you would actually use. Not "I should have
      been clearer". The actual sentence.
  ④ ONE HEDGE I CAUGHT MYSELF USING.
  ⑤ ONE THING I LEARNED THAT COULD BECOME A CARD.
    (file 06 §02)

ONCE A WEEK · 8 MINUTES MORE
  · the recording (§05)
  · one topic card, said out loud (file 06)
  · two words from the questions file (file 16 §06)

⇒ TOTAL: ABOUT FIFTEEN MINUTES A DAY.

⭐ WHY THE EVENING LOG IS NOT OPTIONAL
  It is the FEEDBACK LOOP. Without it you have exposure,
  not practice — and exposure without feedback is what
  produces the twenty-year plateau. Doctors get worse
  with years since training where feedback is absent.
  Meetings work the same way.
  (Ericsson, file 09; file 01 §10)
The Program 04

Your answer bank

In one sentence

Twenty questions you actually face, each with a written forty-five-second answer, said out loud.

Think of it like…

A musician improvising.

They are not inventing from nothing. They have scales, phrases and patterns they have played ten thousand times, and improvisation is recombining them in a new order, fast.

The bank is your scales. It is what makes the improvising possible, and it is exactly the pre-loading argument from file 01 made permanent.

The twenty, and four rules that decide whether it works
3 · WHO YOU ARE
   What you do. What you are known for. What you are
   working on now.
   ⇒ the questions you answer at every introduction and
     still fumble, because you have never once written
     the answer down.

6-8 · YOUR RECORD
   Your canonical stories. EACH ONE WITH A NUMBER IN IT.
   ⇒ if you are preparing for interviews these are
     already drafted in
     ../../supply-chain/phase-0-orientation/03-your-eight-canonical-stories.md
     — the bank is where they get REHEARSED rather than
     just written, which is a different thing.

5 · YOUR NUMBERS
   Five figures you can state exactly, with source and
   date. Volume, cost, rate, growth, headcount —
   whichever five your domain runs on.
   ⇒ one exact number does more for credibility than
     five adjectives. (file 01 §04)

4 · ⭐ THE HARD ONES — WRITE THESE FIRST
   The questions you hope nobody asks. The gap in the
   CV. The project that failed. The thing you do not
   know. The reason you left.
   ⇒ WRITE THESE FIRST because they are the ones that
     cost you when you are unprepared, and because the
     relief of simply having an answer is completely
     disproportionate to the effort. Most of the fear
     is the absence of a sentence, not the fact itself.

5 · YOUR QUESTIONS
   From file 02's bank, rewritten in your own words.

⭐⭐ THE FOUR RULES
  ① WRITE IT OUT LOUD, THEN TRANSCRIBE. NEVER THE
    OTHER WAY ROUND.
    Written language and spoken language have different
    rhythms. An answer composed on paper comes out of
    your mouth sounding composed on paper, and everyone
    can hear it.
  ② FORTY-FIVE SECONDS. TIME IT.
    Over sixty and it is not a bank entry, it is a
    speech, and it will not survive being interrupted.
  ③ EVERY "RECORD" ANSWER CONTAINS ONE NUMBER.
  ④ IT IS A CORE, NOT A SCRIPT.
    ⚠ A MEMORISED SCRIPT IS WORSE THAN NO PREPARATION.
      It makes you rigid, it does not bend to the
      question actually asked, and everyone can hear
      recitation. You are rehearsing the SHAPE and the
      key facts, so that you can adapt them.

⇒ BUILD IT IN WEEKS 2-3. About twenty minutes a day for
  a week. You will use it for years.
The Program 05

The recording

In one sentence

Two minutes of video, once a week, watched back. This is the hardest thing in this library and the most valuable, and those two facts are related.

The protocol · about eight minutes
① PICK A HARD QUESTION
   From your bank, or — better — one you actually
   fumbled this week.

② PHONE, PROPPED UP, ALONE
   No preparation. No second take on the first attempt.
   The point is to capture what you actually do, not
   what you can do on the fourth try.

③ TWO MINUTES. ANSWER IT.

④ WATCH IT. ALL OF IT.
   Not skipping. Not on mute. All of it.

⑤ WRITE THREE LINES
   · one thing that was genuinely fine
   · one hedge or filler I did not know I used
   · one sentence I would rewrite, written out in full

⑥ RE-RECORD THE SAME QUESTION ONCE, WITH THAT ONE
   CHANGE. THEN STOP.
   One change. Not five. Five changes produce a stiff
   performance and no learning.

⭐ WHY IT IS THE MOST VALUABLE THING HERE
  IT IS THE ONLY HONEST FEEDBACK LOOP YOU HAVE.
  Everything else in this library is guesswork about how
  you come across.
  · you cannot hear your own rising inflection
  · you cannot count your own "just"s — everyone
    underestimates their own by a large margin
  · you cannot see yourself trail off at the end of a
    sentence, because from the inside the sentence
    felt finished
  ⇒ nobody will tell you these things. Colleagues do
    not, friends do not, and interviewers certainly do
    not. The camera does, immediately, for free.

⚠ AND A WARNING ABOUT THE FIRST TIME
  YOU WILL HATE IT AND CONCLUDE THAT YOU ARE TERRIBLE.
  Everyone has this reaction. What you are seeing is
  yourself WITHOUT the flattering internal edit that
  normally runs — and the gap between the two is
  startling for every single person who does this.
  ⇒ DO IT A SECOND TIME BEFORE DRAWING ANY CONCLUSIONS.
  ⇒ the discomfort fades after about three sessions and
    does not come back. On the other side of it is the
    only accurate picture of yourself you will ever
    have.
Part III · The eight weeks

One new thing a week

Each week adds one habit and keeps the previous ones. Nothing is dropped, and nothing new is added faster than that on purpose.

The Program 06

Week by week

Weeks 1–4
WEEK 1 · BASELINE AND NOTICE
  · score yourself on the six (§02). WRITE THE DATE
  · daily: category call + evening log
  · one recording
  ⭐ AND DO NOT TRY TO CHANGE ANYTHING THIS WEEK.
    Just notice. You cannot change what you cannot yet
    see, and week one is for seeing. People who start
    fixing in week one fix the wrong things.

WEEK 2 · SUBTRACTION
  everything above, plus:
  · pick ONE hedge — start with "just" — and cut only
    that one. Not all of them. One.
  · begin the answer bank: THE FOUR HARD ONES FIRST
  ⇒ subtraction comes first because it is the fastest
    visible change in the whole programme and it
    requires no new skill. You are removing, not
    learning.

WEEK 3 · STRUCTURE
  plus:
  · every message longer than two lines gets its
    conclusion in the FIRST sentence
  · use "two things —" once a day, deliberately, even
    when you do not need it
  · finish the answer bank

WEEK 4 · RESCORE, AND QUESTIONS
  · SCORE AGAIN. Compare with week 1.
  ⚠ EXPECT SOMETHING TO GO DOWN. Re-read §02 if it
    does. This is the week people quit and this is why.
  plus:
  · ask one question from your bank per day
  · the second-question discipline: one conversation a
    day where you ask a follow-up on the SAME answer
    before changing topic (file 02 §08)
Weeks 5–8
WEEK 5 · PRESENCE
  plus:
  · three full stops a day — downward inflection, a
    real stop, two seconds of silence. No "so, yeah"
  · use "I don't know — I'll have it by [day]" once, on
    purpose, when you could have bluffed. THEN HIT THE
    DATE
  · one warmth signal per meeting: credit someone by
    name, or ask about the thing they mentioned last
    week

WEEK 6 · READING THE ROOM
  plus:
  · before anything contested: five minutes with the
    person most likely to object. "What am I missing?"
    (file 05 §05 — highest hit rate in the library)
  · two lines per person on register: headline or
    detail, written or verbal
  · "thanks for flagging it early" as the FIRST
    sentence whenever anyone brings you a problem

WEEK 7 · SUBSTANCE
  plus:
  · one topic card a day, from ../../culture/ or from
    files 11-16. SAID OUT LOUD
  · ask "what's the part of your job that people always
    get wrong?" three times this week, and then ask the
    second question

WEEK 8 · CONSOLIDATE
  · SCORE AGAIN. Compare weeks 1, 4 and 8.
  · ⭐ READ YOUR EVENING LOGS FROM WEEK ONE.
    This is the part that shows you what actually
    changed — because by now you will have completely
    forgotten what you used to do, which is how
    improvement always feels from the inside.
  · PICK THREE THINGS TO KEEP PERMANENTLY.
    Three. Not eight. A programme you are still running
    in month six is a programme you will abandon in
    month seven.
The Program 07

After week eight

Keep three. Drop the rest. Stop running a programme.

The three that most people find worth keeping, and that survive contact with a busy year:

  • The evening log, cut to two lines. What would I say differently, and one hedge I caught. Ninety seconds.
  • The recording, cut to monthly. Twelve a year is plenty to catch drift.
  • The category call before anything that matters. Two lines, and by now it is mostly automatic anyway.

Plus the two files that keep producing on their own: the questions file (file 16 §06) and the answer bank, updated once a quarter.

And the honest part

You will drift. Everyone drifts. Three months of a busy period and the hedges come back, the logs stop, and the shelf empties.

That is not a character failure and it does not require a new resolution. Re-run week 1 once a quarter — score, log, one recording. It takes a week, it costs almost nothing, and it catches the drift while it is still drift rather than after it has quietly become your baseline.

The Program 08

When you miss

Two rules, and the smallest version that still works
⭐ NEVER MISS TWICE
  Missing one day is an accident. Missing two days is
  the beginning of a different habit.
  ⇒ and the correction is NOT to do double tomorrow.
    It is simply to do the normal thing tomorrow.
    "Catching up" is how people turn a missed day into
    a missed fortnight. (Clear, file 09 §05)

⭐ IF YOU CAN ONLY DO ONE THING ON A BAD DAY, DO THE
  EVENING LOG
  Five lines. Because it is the FEEDBACK LOOP, and that
  is the only part that cannot be replaced by anything
  else. The category call without the log is a guess
  with no answer.

⭐ THE MINIMUM VIABLE PROGRAMME
  If you will not do fifteen minutes — and many people
  will not, honestly — do this instead and do not feel
  bad about it:

    · THE CATEGORY CALL      2 min, morning
    · THE EVENING LOG        5 min
    · ONE RECORDING          monthly

  ⇒ SEVEN MINUTES A DAY AND TWELVE RECORDINGS A YEAR.
  ⇒ that version genuinely works. It is slower and it
    is not nothing, and it is infinitely better than an
    ambitious plan you abandon in week two.
  ⇒ the two-minute rule applies to this file as much as
    to any other habit: SCALE IT DOWN UNTIL YOU WILL
    ACTUALLY DO IT.

That is the library. Sixteen files: six on how to think and speak, three on thirty-two books, six on how the world actually works, and this one to make any of it stick.

If you want to go further: the psychology underneath the first six is in master-yourself/, the knowledge to draw conversation from is in culture/, and the domain depth is in supply-chain/.

On this programme. The structure follows what is reasonably well established about deliberate practice — a specific goal aimed at a weakness, immediate feedback, repetition with refinement, at the edge of ability. The eight-week length, the particular order of the weeks and the six-skill split are this library's own design, not a tested protocol; they are arranged so that the cheapest wins come first and the hardest habit arrives once the easy ones are running. Adapt the schedule. Do not adapt away the evening log or the recording — those two are the feedback, and without feedback the rest is just exposure.
